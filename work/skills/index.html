My skills span IT support, workplace technology, Apple devices, device management, IT operations, networking and automation.

How I apply these skills

Support work is not just a list of tools. I start by clarifying what the user was trying to do, establish whether the symptoms follow a user, device or service, and choose a low-risk check that can distinguish likely causes. After a change, I validate the original task, record the evidence and identify when a specialist or service owner needs to take over.

The examples below connect selected skill areas to repeatable, public-safe workflows. They show my approach to troubleshooting, device support, networking and small automation—not a claim of equal depth across every listed product or a replacement for vendor documentation and employer procedures.

From skill label to observable support work

A skill is most useful when it changes the next safe action. These examples show what I would try to establish and where a reader can explore a related public workflow. They are learning examples, not records of a particular employer incident.

Skill areaObservable support practiceRelated guide
Service desk and incident handlingRecord the user goal, impact, scope, exact symptom and last known-good point; test one low-risk hypothesis and verify the original task after a change.Troubleshooting workflow
Apple and device managementConfirm device ownership, enrollment context and expected management state before changing settings; pause when the authorized workflow is unclear.Enrollment models
Identity and collaborationSeparate sign-in failure from permission, application and service availability; capture the exact error and compare with an approved unaffected path.Sign-in troubleshooting
Networking and VPNCheck whether the issue follows one device, network or service; use ordered checks and avoid treating one successful ping as proof that the full workflow works.Connectivity checklist
Platforms and ticket documentationKeep a time-ordered record of observations, tests, results, changes and the next owner so another technician can continue without repeating unsafe steps.Ticket documentation
Automation and scriptingStart with a narrow, reversible task; test on sample input, inspect output and define failure handling before using it in a real workflow.Small automation guide

These practices describe a way to approach support work; they do not imply administrator access to every platform or equal depth across every product listed above.
