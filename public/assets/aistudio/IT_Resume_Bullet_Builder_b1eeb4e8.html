How to write strong IT resume bullets

Recruiters and applicant tracking systems (ATS) scan IT resumes for three things: what you did, which technology you used, and what changed because of it. A bullet that covers all three is far more useful than a list of duties.

The formula: Action verb + task + tools + scale + result. Example: Configured VLAN segmentation on Cisco Meraki switches for 250 users across 3 sites, reducing network incidents by 35%.

  • Start with a specific past-tense verb. "Deployed", "Diagnosed" and "Migrated" say more than "Responsible for".
  • Name the technology. ATS filters match tools such as Active Directory, VMware, Intune or Cisco exactly, so use the same spelling as the job posting.
  • Add numbers you can verify. Users supported, devices managed, tickets closed, uptime, minutes saved, sites covered.
  • Keep it to one or two lines. Aim for 8–28 words.
  • Never invent results. If you do not have a number, leave it out or write what you can honestly support. This tool marks missing information as "Not Provided" instead of guessing.

Before and after

  • Before: Responsible for fixing printers and helping users with laptops.
  • After: Diagnosed and repaired laptops and network printers for 120 staff, keeping first-day resolution at [your verified figure].
  • Before: Worked on server backups.
  • After: Automated nightly Veeam backups for 14 Windows Server VMs and tested monthly restores.

FAQ

Does this tool use AI or send my text anywhere?

No. It runs entirely in your browser using fixed templates and checks. Your text is not uploaded. Saved bullets stay in your own browser storage.

What if I have no numbers?

Use scale instead: number of users, devices, sites or tickets. If even that is unknown, describe the scope honestly, for example "supported a 40-person office".

How many bullets per job?

Three to six for recent roles, fewer for older ones. Put the most job-relevant bullet first.

Will this guarantee interviews?

No. It helps your resume read clearly to people and ATS software, but hiring decisions depend on many factors outside any tool.
