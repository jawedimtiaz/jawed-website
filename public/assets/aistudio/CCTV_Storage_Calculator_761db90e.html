How CCTV storage is calculated

Video storage depends on how much data each camera produces every second. The formula is simple:

GB per camera per day = bitrate (Mbps) × 86,400 seconds ÷ 8 ÷ 1,000. For example, a 4 Mbps stream uses 4 × 86,400 ÷ 8 ÷ 1,000 = 43.2 GB per day. Multiply by the number of cameras and days of retention, then add headroom.

  • Bitrate is the biggest unknown. The presets are typical starting points only. Real bitrate depends on frame rate, scene motion, lighting, bitrate mode and the camera brand. Read the actual figure from your camera's web page or the recorder's status screen and type it in.
  • Motion recording reduces storage only if the scene is quiet. Busy scenes can record almost all day.
  • Headroom of 10 to 20% covers bitrate spikes at night, audio, and the recorder's own overhead.
  • RAID trades capacity for protection. RAID 5 loses one drive's capacity, RAID 6 two, and RAID 1 or 10 half. RAID is not a backup. Surveillance-rated drives are recommended.
  • Drive makers use decimal terabytes (1 TB = 1,000 GB), which matches this calculator. Operating systems may show slightly less.

Results are estimates for planning. Confirm the final design with your recorder's capacity tool and the actual camera settings.

FAQ

Is anything uploaded?

No. Everything is calculated in your browser.

Why is my real usage different?

Variable bitrate, scene activity, frame rate, audio and recorder overhead all change the result. Measure a few days of real usage and adjust the bitrate.

Which RAID should I use for CCTV?

RAID 5 is common for balance, and RAID 6 is safer with many large drives. Always check your recorder's supported modes.
