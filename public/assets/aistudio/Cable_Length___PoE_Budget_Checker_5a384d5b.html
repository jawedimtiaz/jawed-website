Ethernet cable limits and PoE power, explained

Cable length. For twisted-pair Ethernet the standard channel limit is 100 metres (328 ft): up to 90 m of permanent cable in the wall plus up to 10 m of patch cords in total. Cat5e and above support 1 Gbps at 100 m. 2.5 Gbps works on Cat5e, 5 Gbps needs Cat6, and 10 Gbps needs Cat6a for the full 100 m. Cat6 can carry 10 Gbps only over shorter runs, commonly up to 55 m and sometimes as little as 37 m in noisy cable bundles. Cat5e is not rated for 10 Gbps.

PoE standards. PoE delivers power over the same cable. The switch (the power source) reserves power by device class, not by what the device actually draws.

  • Add up the class watts of every device and compare with the switch's total PoE budget, which is usually lower than ports × port power.
  • Many installers keep total load under about 80% of the budget for headroom. This is a planning habit, not a standard.
  • Many devices draw less than their class maximum, but plan for the worst case, especially for cameras with heaters or IR and for PTZ units.
  • Use solid-copper cable for PoE runs. Copper-clad aluminium (CCA) cable runs hot, loses power and is not standards-compliant.

Always confirm with the manufacturers' datasheets and local installation rules. These results are planning estimates.

FAQ

Does this send my data anywhere?

No. Everything runs in your browser.

Can I go over 100 m?

Not reliably on copper. Use a switch or PoE extender partway, or switch to fibre.

Do all devices use their full class power?

No, but the switch may reserve the full class amount, so plan with the class value.
