New vs Old Tax Regime: How to Think About the Comparison

A practical framework for comparing the two individual income-tax regimes for AY 2026-27 without assuming that one regime is automatically right for everyone.

Published: 2026-09-28 · Updated: 2026-09-28 · 6 min read

The comparison starts with two different structures

For AY 2026-27, the new tax regime under section 115BAC is the default regime for eligible individuals. Eligible taxpayers can opt out and use the old regime, subject to the applicable rules. The Income Tax Department describes the old regime as retaining various deductions and exemptions, while the default regime has lower slab rates with fewer deductions and exemptions.

This means a useful comparison should consider both the tax-rate structure and the deductions or exemptions actually available to the taxpayer.

The table is a simplified comparison of the headline slabs for an individual below 60 years of age. It should not be used by itself to calculate final tax because rebates, special-rate income, deductions, surcharge and cess can affect the result.

Why deductions matter

The old regime allows a wider range of deductions and exemptions subject to their conditions. The new regime permits only specified deductions and exemptions. For example, the Income Tax Department identifies employer contributions to NPS under section 80CCD(2) as a deduction available under the new regime, subject to the applicable limit and conditions.

Therefore, the relevant question is not simply “Which slab is lower?” A comparison should ask how much taxable income remains after the deductions and exemptions available under each regime.

A neutral comparison worksheet

  1. Start with the same gross income and income sources for both calculations.
  2. Separate income that may be taxed at special rates from ordinary slab-rate income.
  3. Apply only the deductions and exemptions permitted under the selected regime.
  4. Calculate taxable income under the old regime.
  5. Calculate taxable income under the new regime.
  6. Apply the relevant slab rates and any applicable special-rate provisions.
  7. Apply eligible rebates, surcharge and health and education cess.
  8. Compare the resulting tax liability rather than comparing headline rates alone.

Rebates can materially change a comparison

For AY 2026-27, the Income Tax Department states that a resident individual under the new regime can receive a rebate of up to ₹60,000 where taxable income does not exceed ₹12,00,000, subject to the applicable conditions. The old regime has its own rebate rules and thresholds. A comparison therefore needs to include the applicable rebate rather than stopping after the slab calculation.

Regime choice and filing context

For non-business cases, the Income Tax Department states that the option to change from the default new regime can generally be exercised each year directly in the income-tax return, filed by the applicable due date. Taxpayers with eligible business or professional income have additional Form 10-IEA requirements and restrictions around changing regimes.

An employee may also need to communicate the intended regime to the employer for TDS purposes during the year. The Income Tax Department notes that if no intimation is made, the default new regime is presumed for employer TDS purposes. This TDS treatment should not be confused with the final regime choice available when filing the return.

Use the official calculator for the final comparison

The Income Tax Department provides an Income and Tax Calculator that can compare tax under the old and new regimes using inputs such as income, deductions, taxpayer category, age and residential status. Using the official calculator is preferable to relying on a simplified article when preparing an actual tax calculation.

Important limitations

This framework is intentionally educational. It does not calculate an individual's tax liability and does not account for every special-rate income, deduction, exemption, surcharge rule or filing condition. Tax rules can change, so the applicable financial year, assessment year and current official instructions should always be checked.
