US Stocks and ETFs: A Practical Starting Point

A simple framework for understanding individual companies and exchange-traded funds before thinking about portfolio construction.

Published: 2026-09-28 · Updated: 2026-09-28 · 6 min read

Start with the exposure

A stock represents an ownership interest in a company. An ETF is a pooled investment product that can hold stocks, bonds or other assets and trades on an exchange. Start by asking what exposure you want rather than which ticker looks attractive.

Individual stocks

Owning one company creates concentrated exposure to that business. Research should consider the business model, financial condition, competitive position, valuation and risks. A good company can still be unsuitable at an unsuitable price.

ETFs and index funds

Many ETFs provide exposure to a basket of securities. Index funds are designed to track an index. A broad fund can simplify diversification, but a narrowly focused ETF is not automatically diversified.

Comparison checklist

  • Exposure: What does it actually hold?
  • Concentration: How dependent is the result on a small number of holdings?
  • Costs: What fees and transaction costs apply?
  • Risk: What could cause a material loss?
  • Time horizon: How long can the money remain invested?
  • Portfolio role: What does this add that the rest of the portfolio does not?

Diversification

Diversification spreads exposure across investments rather than depending on one company or narrow segment. It can reduce concentration risk, but it cannot prevent losses when markets broadly decline.
