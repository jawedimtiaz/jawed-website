Portfolio Allocation and Rebalancing: A Practical Framework

A practical way to think about investment roles, allocation drift, diversification and portfolio reviews.

Published: 2026-09-28 · Updated: 2026-09-28 · 6 min read

Allocation comes before product selection

Portfolio allocation is the decision about how much of a portfolio is exposed to different asset classes or investment roles. The appropriate mix depends on the goal, time horizon and risk tolerance; there is no universal allocation that fits everyone.

Investor.gov: Asset Allocation and Diversification

Think in roles

Instead of treating every holding as an isolated decision, assign each major allocation a role. A growth allocation, liquidity allocation or diversification allocation can then be evaluated by what it is supposed to do for the overall plan.

Measure drift

Market movements change portfolio weights. A simple review compares current weight with intended weight and records the difference. This creates an objective starting point for deciding whether a rebalance is needed.

Rebalancing is a rule, not a prediction

Rebalancing restores a portfolio toward its intended structure. A policy can use a calendar review, a percentage-band trigger, or a combination. The useful part is defining the rule before recent performance or emotion drives the decision.

Review checklist

  1. What is the goal and time horizon?
  2. What role does each major allocation serve?
  3. What is the current percentage allocation?
  4. How far has each allocation moved from its intended range?
  5. Has the underlying investment thesis or role changed?
  6. Can new contributions correct part of the drift without selling?
  7. What taxes, fees or trading constraints should be considered?

What this framework does not do

It does not predict markets or identify a universally correct target allocation. It is a structure for making portfolio reviews more deliberate and repeatable.
