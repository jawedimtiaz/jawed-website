Inflation and Future Goal Planning

A goal measured in today's rupees may require a different nominal amount in the future because purchasing power changes over time.

Published: 2026-09-28 · Updated: 2026-09-28 · 5 min read

Why today's price is not enough

If a future goal is several years away, using today's price without an inflation assumption can understate the nominal amount that may be needed. This is particularly relevant for goals such as education, a home purchase, a major family expense or another planned future outlay.

The basic calculation

If today's goal cost is C, the planning inflation rate is i, and the goal is n years away:

Future goal cost = C × (1 + i)n

For example, a hypothetical ₹10 lakh goal ten years away at 6% assumed annual inflation becomes approximately ₹17.91 lakh in nominal terms. This demonstrates the formula; it is not an inflation forecast.

Goal-specific inflation can differ

Not every expense category changes at the same rate. Education, healthcare, housing, technology and everyday consumption can experience different price movements. A broad inflation measure can therefore be a useful planning reference without being a precise forecast for a particular goal.

Keep the assumption visible

A useful planning model should show the inflation rate and the number of years rather than hiding them inside a single target number. If either assumption changes, the future nominal goal amount changes too.

This also makes scenario analysis simple: compare a lower, base and higher inflation assumption instead of treating one rate as certain.

Do not confuse inflation with investment return

Inflation answers a purchasing-power question: how much might the same basket of goods cost in the future under the chosen assumption? Investment return answers a different question: how might an amount of money change if it is invested?

A complete savings plan may eventually model both, but they should remain separate assumptions so the effect of each is visible.

A simple goal-planning sequence

  1. Define the goal and its cost today.
  2. Estimate how many years remain.
  3. Choose an explicit planning inflation assumption.
  4. Calculate the future nominal goal cost.
  5. List money already earmarked for the goal separately.
  6. Only then consider how future contributions or investment returns might help fund the target.
  7. Review the goal when its expected cost, date or assumptions change.
