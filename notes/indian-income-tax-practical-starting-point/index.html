Indian Income Tax: A Practical Starting Point

An educational starting point for understanding individual income tax in India, using the rules published for Assessment Year (AY) 2026-27.

Published: 2026-09-28 · Updated: 2026-09-28 · 6 min read

Start by separating FY and AY

Indian tax material often refers to two different periods. The Financial Year (FY) is the year in which income is earned. The Assessment Year (AY) is the following year in which that income is assessed and the return is filed. This note uses AY 2026-27, which relates to income for FY 2025-26.

Understand the two tax regimes

For AY 2026-27, the new tax regime under section 115BAC is the default regime for eligible individuals. Eligible taxpayers can opt for the old tax regime instead, subject to the applicable rules. The two regimes differ in tax rates and in the deductions and exemptions available.

For individuals without income from business or profession, the Income Tax Department states that the choice can generally be changed each year through the income-tax return, subject to the applicable filing rules. Taxpayers with business or professional income have additional conditions for opting out of the default regime.

AY 2026-27: new-regime slab structure

For individuals below 60 years of age during the previous year, the Income Tax Department lists these new-regime slab rates for AY 2026-27:

Above ₹24,00,00030%

These are slab rates, not a single rate applied to the entire taxable income. Other rules, including rebates, surcharge where applicable and health and education cess, can affect the final tax payable.

Rebate under section 87A

For AY 2026-27, the Income Tax Department states that a resident individual under the new regime can receive a rebate of up to ₹60,000 where taxable income does not exceed ₹12,00,000, subject to the applicable conditions. The rebate is a tax calculation provision; it does not mean that every form of income is automatically tax-free.

Old regime: think in terms of deductions as well as rates

The old regime uses different slab rates and retains access to a wider range of deductions and exemptions, subject to their individual conditions. A meaningful comparison therefore requires more than comparing headline slab rates: it requires calculating taxable income under each regime using the deductions and exemptions actually available to the taxpayer.

Taxable income is not the same as salary or total receipts

A practical tax review should identify the relevant income sources and then apply the rules for each source. Depending on the taxpayer, these can include salary, interest, dividends, capital gains, rental income and other income. The calculation can also involve deductions, exemptions, rebates, surcharge and cess. This is why a gross salary figure alone is not enough to determine final tax liability.

A simple annual tax-review sequence

  1. Identify the applicable financial year and assessment year.
  2. List income sources and relevant tax documents.
  3. Determine whether the new or old regime applies and whether an alternative regime can be chosen.
  4. Calculate taxable income under the relevant rules.
  5. Apply slab rates and any applicable special-rate provisions.
  6. Check rebates, surcharge and health and education cess.
  7. Compare tax already paid through TDS, advance tax or other applicable payments with the final liability.
  8. File the appropriate income-tax return using the current official instructions.

Keep tax information date-specific

Tax rules can change through Finance Acts, notifications, circulars and updated return instructions. This note is deliberately tied to AY 2026-27 rather than presenting its figures as permanent rules. Before acting on a tax matter, check the latest Income Tax Department material applicable to the relevant year and your circumstances.
