Health Insurance: Understanding the Basics

Health insurance is a risk-transfer tool. The useful starting point is understanding what a policy covers, what it does not cover, and how the policy's cost-sharing and claim conditions work.

Published: 2026-09-28 · Updated: 2026-09-28 · 8 min read

What health insurance is designed to do

A health insurance policy can help transfer specified healthcare costs to an insurer in return for a premium, subject to the policy's terms, conditions, exclusions, limits and applicable regulations. The policy document—not an advertisement or a summary alone—is the key reference for understanding the contract.

IRDAI's health-insurance framework and policyholder-protection rules provide the regulatory context, while individual products can still differ substantially in coverage and conditions.

Five terms to understand first

TermWhat to check
Sum insuredThe policy's stated coverage limit, subject to the product's terms and sub-limits.
Waiting periodHow long you may need to wait before specified conditions or treatments become covered.
ExclusionA condition, treatment, circumstance or expense that the policy does not cover.
Co-paymentThe percentage or amount of an eligible claim that the policyholder must bear under the policy.
DeductibleAn amount that must be paid before the insurer's coverage applies, where the policy includes one.

These terms should be read together. A large sum insured does not by itself mean every medical expense will be paid.

Waiting periods and pre-existing diseases

Waiting periods vary by policy and condition. IRDAI's current health-insurer guidance states that where a disclosed pre-existing disease is accepted, it is covered after the waiting period specified in the policy, with the waiting period subject to the applicable regulatory limit. IRDAI currently states a maximum of 48 months for such a waiting period.

This makes accurate disclosure at proposal stage important. A policyholder should read the definition of “pre-existing disease” and the waiting-period wording in the actual policy rather than assuming that every existing condition is immediately covered.

Exclusions matter as much as coverage

Exclusions identify situations or expenses that are not covered. They can include permanent exclusions, specific treatments, non-medical expenses or other conditions depending on the product and applicable rules.

When comparing policies, a useful checklist is:

  • Which treatments or conditions are excluded?
  • Are there disease-specific or treatment-specific limits?
  • Are there room-rent or category restrictions?
  • Are there co-payments or deductibles?
  • Are there waiting periods?
  • Which non-medical expenses are excluded?

Cashless and reimbursement claims

Health policies can use cashless treatment arrangements at eligible network facilities and reimbursement processes for covered expenses, subject to the policy and applicable claim procedures. The exact process, documentation and timelines should be checked in the policy and insurer's current claim guidance.

A practical habit is to keep the policy schedule, policy wording, insurer contact details, claim instructions and relevant medical records accessible before a claim is needed.

Renewal is part of the plan

Health insurance is not only a purchase decision; it is an ongoing protection arrangement. At renewal, review whether the insured people, coverage amount, exclusions, waiting periods, cost-sharing provisions, network information and policy terms still match your needs.

IRDAI has also issued policyholder-protection requirements and health-insurance business rules that have changed over time. Current regulatory material should be checked when a policy is renewed or materially changed.

Employer cover and personal cover are different planning layers

Employer-provided health insurance can be an important benefit, but its continuation and terms depend on the employment arrangement and policy. A personal policy is a separate contract with its own terms. A household can therefore evaluate employer cover and personal cover as separate layers rather than assuming that one automatically replaces the other.

A simple policy-review checklist

  1. Confirm who is insured and the policy period.
  2. Record the sum insured and any restoration or related features described in the policy.
  3. List waiting periods and pre-existing-condition provisions.
  4. Review exclusions and permanent exclusions.
  5. Check co-payment, deductible and sub-limit provisions.
  6. Understand cashless and reimbursement claim procedures.
  7. Confirm the insurer's current support and grievance channels.
  8. Review the policy wording at renewal rather than relying only on the premium notice.
  9. Keep copies of the policy schedule and relevant documents.

What this note does not do

This note does not compare insurers, rank policies, recommend a sum insured or determine whether a particular policy is suitable for a household. Insurance needs depend on personal circumstances, existing coverage, financial capacity, medical and family considerations, policy terms and current regulations.
