Indian Mutual Funds, Index Funds and ETFs: A Practical Starting Point

A simple educational framework for understanding three common ways to obtain market exposure in India.

Published: 2026-09-28 · Updated: 2026-09-28 · 6 min read

Start with the structure

A mutual fund pools money from investors and invests according to its stated objective. An index fund aims to track a benchmark index. An ETF also provides pooled exposure but its units trade on a stock exchange during market hours.

The structure matters because it affects how you buy, sell, value and monitor the investment.

Active mutual funds

An actively managed fund gives a fund manager discretion over portfolio selection within the scheme's stated mandate. The relevant comparison is not simply whether the fund made money, but how its results, costs, risks and portfolio construction fit the objective.

Index funds

An index fund seeks to track a chosen index. This makes the benchmark central to understanding the investment: know what the index contains, how it is constructed and what exposure you are actually getting.

ETFs

ETFs trade on exchanges and their market price can move during the trading day. In addition to the underlying portfolio, an investor should understand liquidity, trading costs and the relationship between market price and net asset value.

AMFI describes ETFs as exchange-traded products that can track an index, commodity, bonds or a basket of assets. AMFI's investor education material provides further background.

A practical comparison

  • Objective: What is the fund or ETF designed to do?
  • Benchmark: If it tracks an index, what index?
  • Portfolio: What does it actually hold?
  • Costs: What expenses and transaction costs apply?
  • Tracking: For passive products, how closely does the product follow its benchmark?
  • Liquidity: How easily can you buy or sell at a reasonable price?

Don't compare products without comparing roles

Two funds can look similar while serving different roles, and two products can look different while providing overlapping exposure. Start with the asset allocation or portfolio role, then compare products that can perform that role.
