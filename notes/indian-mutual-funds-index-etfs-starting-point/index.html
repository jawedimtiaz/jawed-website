Indian Mutual Funds, Index Funds and ETFs: A Practical Starting Point

A simple educational framework for understanding three common ways to obtain market exposure in India.

Published: 2026-09-28 · Updated: 2026-09-28 · 6 min read

Start with the structure

A mutual fund pools money from investors and invests according to its stated objective. An index fund aims to track a benchmark. An ETF also provides pooled exposure, but its units trade on a stock exchange during market hours.

Active mutual funds

An actively managed fund gives a fund manager discretion over portfolio selection within the scheme mandate. Compare its objective, portfolio, costs, risks and results rather than looking at returns alone.

Index funds

An index fund seeks to track a chosen index. The benchmark is therefore central: understand what the index contains, how it is constructed and what exposure you actually receive.

ETFs

ETFs trade on exchanges and their market price changes during the trading day. Consider the underlying portfolio, liquidity, trading costs and the relationship between market price and net asset value.

AMFI investor education: mutual funds, index funds and ETFs

Practical comparison

  • Objective: What is the product designed to do?
  • Benchmark: If passive, which index does it track?
  • Portfolio: What does it actually hold?
  • Costs: What expenses and transaction costs apply?
  • Tracking: How closely does a passive product follow its benchmark?
  • Liquidity: How easily can it be bought or sold at a reasonable price?

Compare roles, not just products

Two products can look similar while serving different roles, and different products can create overlapping exposure. Start with the portfolio role, then compare products that can perform that role.
