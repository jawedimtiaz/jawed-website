Money Basics: Budgeting, Emergency Funds and Net Worth

A simple framework for understanding today's cash flow, preparing for unexpected costs and tracking long-term progress.

Published: 2026-09-28 · Updated: 2026-10-12 · 7 min read

Start with cash flow

Understand income and spending over a realistic period. Separate recurring costs from irregular expenses and include savings contributions as part of the plan. A budget is a way to see whether current cash flow supports the goals you have chosen.

Build an emergency reserve

An emergency fund is money set aside for unplanned expenses or financial shocks. The appropriate amount depends on circumstances such as income stability, essential expenses, dependents and access to other resources.

Reserve Bank of India: Financial Education — emergency fund guidance

Track net worth

Net worth is a snapshot of assets minus liabilities. It can help show whether your overall financial position is changing, but it should be viewed alongside cash flow, liquidity, debt obligations and financial goals.

Net worth = Total assets − Total liabilities

Simple monthly review

  1. Record income received.
  2. Review essential and discretionary spending.
  3. Check savings and emergency reserves.
  4. Review debt and upcoming irregular expenses.
  5. Update net worth periodically.
  6. Check progress toward important goals.

Illustrative monthly cash-flow example

Fictional example only. These figures are not a suggested budget or a target for every household.

Monthly itemExample amountWhy track it?
Take-home income₹60,000Start with money actually received, after payroll deductions.
Essential costs₹35,000Housing, food, utilities, transport and other necessary commitments.
Flexible spending₹10,000Discretionary purchases that may vary by month.
Saving and debt reduction₹15,000Show how current cash flow is allocated to reserves, goals or liabilities.

The useful result is not whether the example's proportions are ideal. It is whether the categories match actual spending, whether irregular expenses are included, and whether the plan leaves enough liquidity for near-term obligations.

Estimate an emergency reserve

Start with essential monthly living costs, not every discretionary expense. Reserve needs depend on income stability, dependants, insurance, debt commitments and access to other resources. RBI financial-education material describes a reserve covering at least three months of living expenses as a general starting point, with a larger buffer potentially appropriate when income is less secure. Treat this as a planning reference, not a rule for every household.

If essential costs in a fictional example are ₹35,000 per month, three months would equal ₹1,05,000. A six-month scenario would equal ₹2,10,000. The figures simply show the arithmetic; choose a target based on your own circumstances and keep the reserve accessible.

Net-worth example

Suppose a fictional household lists ₹8 lakh in assets and ₹3 lakh in liabilities. Its net worth at that date is ₹5 lakh:

Net worth = ₹8,00,000 − ₹3,00,000 = ₹5,00,000

That snapshot does not show whether the household can pay next month's bills. Track cash flow and accessible savings separately from total net worth, which may include assets that are not quickly available.

Keep the system maintainable

A financial system that takes too much effort is difficult to maintain. Start with a small number of categories and add detail only when it changes a decision.
